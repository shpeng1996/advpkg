# Biaxial bending strength of TGV glass substrates in ring-on-ring tests

## Abstract (reconstructed from OpenAlex inverted index)

> Glass substrates and glass interposers are emerging as promising alternatives to organic substrates and silicon interposers in advanced IC packaging for AI, HPC, and 5G/6G applications. This is due to their tunable CTE, high stiffness, excellent electrical performance, low cost, scalability, and compatibility with fine-line RDLs. However, the **intrinsic brittleness and crack susceptibility of glass materials constitute major obstacles to their widespread adoption**. This study will investigate the mechanics of the through-glass-via (TGV) array in the biaxial bending ring-on-ring (RoR) test using theoretical and numerical stress analyses, and then **experimentally determine the bending strength of TGV glass substrates with and without the etching process**. The experimentally obtained bending strengths are analyzed and discussed in relation to the corresponding failure modes and underlying mechanical behavior.

## What it contributes

- Treats the **TGV array itself** as the mechanical object of study — stress concentration around the via array under **biaxial** (ring-on-ring) loading, rather than the uniaxial 3- or 4-point bend usually quoted for glass coupons.
- Separates the contribution of the **etching process** by testing substrates with and without it, tying strength directly to a process step a manufacturer controls.
- Combines closed-form stress analysis, FEA and experiment, with failure modes mapped to the measured strengths.

## Why it matters

The wiki's existing glass-substrate thesis holds that the industrialisation bottleneck sits at **edges and singulation rather than at TGV formation**. This paper is the first source in the wiki to put the **TGV array itself** back under mechanical scrutiny, and to attribute strength loss to the **etch** rather than only to edge damage — a possible qualification of that thesis rather than a confirmation of it.
