# Sidewall passivation in single-step non-Bosch deep-Si etch (IBM)

## Abstract (reconstructed from OpenAlex inverted index)

> Bosch processes involving C₄F₈ and SF₆ have been conventionally used for through-silicon via etch. However, given the **high global warming potential of C₄F₈** and the importance of high aspect-ratio deep-Si etch processes for packaging applications in next-generation technology nodes, it is of great interest to explore alternative plasma chemistries focusing on sustainable process development in the future. In our previous work, we reported the feasibility of using **CH₄ and C₄F₆ gas mixtures in lieu of C₄F₈, and BCl₃ as an etchant additive along with SF₆ in a single-step non-Bosch system**. Parametric studies for throughput improvement were conducted, and "transition" discontinuities were observed for which a formation mechanism was proposed. In this work, the role of BCl₃ as an additive in CH₄-C₄F₆-BCl₃-SF₆ gas mixtures is discussed and further characterization on the sidewall composition is presented using **time-of-flight secondary-ion mass spectrometry**. The addition of BCl₃ showed **significant reduction in the F:C ratio of the polymer film** as measured by **x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy**, with the effect being more pronounced in the areas subject to ion bombardment.

## Key findings

- **BCl₃ addition lowers the F:C ratio** of the sidewall passivation polymer, and the effect is stronger where ion bombardment is strongest — giving a knob for depth-dependent sidewall control.
- A **single-step** (non-Bosch, non-alternating) chemistry for high-aspect-ratio deep-Si etch, which removes the scalloping inherent to Bosch cycling.
- Characterisation by ToF-SIMS and XPS.

## Why it matters

Two threads the wiki has not connected. First, **TSV etch process control**: single-step etch changes sidewall profile and therefore liner/barrier conformality and TSV reliability. Second, and more structurally, the driver here is **environmental compliance, not performance** — C₄F₈ has a high GWP, and the fluorinated-gas and PFAS regulatory pressure that produced Fujifilm's PFAS-free PBO (collected 2026-09-15) is now reaching the **via etch step** at an IDM research lab. That makes it the second independent instance this quarter of environmental regulation reshaping a core advanced-packaging unit process.
