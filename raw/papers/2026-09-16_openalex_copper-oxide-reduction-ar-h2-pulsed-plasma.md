# Reduction behavior and mechanism of copper oxide in Ar/H₂ pulsed plasma

## Abstract (reconstructed from OpenAlex inverted index)

> Copper (Cu) is widely utilized as a core material in various next-generation electronic device processes, including semiconductor interconnects, Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs), micro-bumps, and hybrid bonding. However, oxide layers formed by air exposure or during processing act as major factors causing increased electrical contact resistance, reduced interfacial bonding strength, hindered elemental diffusion, and degraded device reliability. In this study, the reduction behavior of copper oxide films was analyzed using an Ar/H₂ pulsed plasma process and the effects of plasma power and gas composition on the reduction characteristics were systematically evaluated. Reduction treatment was carried out on copper samples prepared under different oxidation conditions, and the behavior of plasma active species during the process was analyzed in real time by optical emission spectroscopy (OES). Furthermore, changes in the surface characteristics of the treated samples were investigated in detail using spectrophotometer-based color difference analysis and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). As a result, the behavior of active species in the plasma changed distinctly according to the On/Off cycle of hydrogen supply and this behavior showed a close correlation with the actual reduction characteristics of the samples. In addition, the reduction efficiency and surface state exhibited different tendencies depending on the applied power and gas composition, thereby clarifying a process mechanism governed by the combined effects of physical surface activation and chemical reduction reactions.

## Key findings

- Plasma active-species behaviour tracks the **H₂ On/Off pulse cycle**, and that behaviour correlates with measured reduction of the Cu oxide — i.e. pulsing is not just a thermal-load trick, it changes the chemistry.
- Reduction efficiency and final **surface state** move *differently* with applied power and gas composition: the mechanism is a **combination of physical surface activation and chemical reduction**, and the two can be tuned against each other.
- Diagnostics: in-situ OES; ex-situ colour-difference spectrophotometry and XPS.

## Why it matters

Quantifies the **pre-bond surface preparation** step that both hybrid bonding and microbump joining depend on, and which the wiki has so far treated as a single undifferentiated "surface activation" axis.
