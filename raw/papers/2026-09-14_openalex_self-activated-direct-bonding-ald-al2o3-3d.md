# Self-Activated Direct Bonding with a Highly Polar Atomic-Layer-Deposited Film for 3D Integration

**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c08143
**Published**: 2026-09-03
**Venue**: ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (ACS, high-impact journal)
**Authors**: Hayato Kitagawa, Taisuke Yamamoto, Jenyu Lee
**Institutions**: Yokohama National University; Mitsubishi Electric (Japan)

## Abstract

We report a previously unknown bonding regime: "self-activated, plasma-free" direct wafer bonding, enabled by atomic-layer-deposited (ALD) Al2O3 on 300-mm wafers. Direct bonding underpins all advanced three-dimensional (3D) complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) and heterogeneous chiplet architectures, yet dielectric surfaces have been assumed to require aggressive plasma activation or chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) to achieve sufficient surface energy for room-temperature bonding. This work demonstrates that a thin ALD Al2O3 film provides sufficient surface polarity and hydroxyl density to achieve void-free direct bonding without plasma treatment, reducing process complexity and potential surface damage.

## Key Findings

- **Self-activated plasma-free bonding**: ALD Al2O3 on 300-mm wafers achieves room-temperature bonding without plasma activation
- **Process simplification**: Eliminates plasma activation step, reducing cost and surface damage risk
- **Surface mechanism**: ALD Al2O3 high polarity + hydroxyl density enables void-free bonding
- **Wafer scale**: Demonstrated on 300-mm wafers (production-relevant)
- **Relevance**: Directly applicable to Cu-Cu hybrid bonding at sub-µm pitch by simplifying the dielectric bonding step

## Wiki Significance

This paper introduces a potentially disruptive simplification to hybrid bonding process flow: eliminating plasma activation through ALD Al2O3 film chemistry. Current hybrid bonding (SoIC-X, Foveros Direct) requires precise plasma activation for dielectric-dielectric bonding — a yield-sensitive step. If confirmed at production scale, this ALD approach could reduce yield loss from plasma non-uniformity, especially critical as pitches approach 1 µm.
