As demand grows for high-value memory used in servers and AI, SK hynix is accelerating its shift to more advanced DRAM. According to Chosun Biz, SK hynix is rapidly expanding production of its sixth-generation 10nm-class (1c) DRAM, which is set to become the core memory die for its next-generation HBM4E. The transition comes as SK hynix prepares HBM4E for mass production in 2027. The company said in July that it had already provided HBM4E samples to customers as development moves forward.

## 1c DRAM Production Ramp Trajectory

Sources cited by Chosun Biz say 1c DRAM accounted for around 10% of SK hynix's production in 1Q26 and 13% in 2Q26, compared with roughly 16% at Samsung and 19% at Micron. SK hynix's share is projected to climb:
- 3Q26: ~24%
- 4Q26: ~34% (surpassing Samsung's ~31%)
- 1Q27: ~35%, overtaking 1b (~33%) for the first time as main DRAM process

SK hynix also said during its 2Q26 earnings call that shipments of 1c DRAM began ramping up during the quarter. Looking to the second half, the company expects stronger bit growth as HBM4 volumes increase. Shipments of 1c-based general-purpose DRAM are also expected to expand.

## Samsung vs. SK hynix HBM4 Strategy Divergence

Samsung and SK hynix are taking different approaches to HBM4:
- **Samsung**: Adopted 1c DRAM for HBM4, targeting speeds of around 11.7Gbps
- **SK hynix**: Prioritized mass-production stability with proven 1b DRAM and MR-MUF packaging

SK hynix is maintaining its HBM4 volume lead with its conservative approach, while Samsung is seeking to gain share through stronger performance.

## 1c Shift Benefits: Productivity and Cost

SK hynix's accelerated shift to 1c is expected to bring benefits in cost and productivity — by increasing the number of bits produced per wafer, the newer process could improve cost competitiveness and help support DRAM profitability from the second half onward. An industry official cautioned that the transition will only be meaningful if mass-production yields improve and customer qualification schedules keep pace.

## Competition Extends to 1d DRAM (HBM5E Generation)

Competition is already extending to seventh-generation 10nm-class (1d) DRAM:
- **Samsung**: Targeting 1d development completion in September 2026, mass production prep in December 2026; initial mass production ~end of 2027; plans to use 1d in HBM5E (~2030)
- **SK hynix**: Targeting 1d development completion in December 2026, mass production prep June 2027 (6 months behind Samsung); production start pushed further out

Samsung plans to use 1d DRAM in HBM5E, expected to launch around 2030. SK hynix has yet to disclose its 1d application plans.
