# Advancing Heterogeneous Integration Through Industry Roadmap Improvements

**Published**: May 21, 2026 | **Author**: Ravi V. Mahajan (Intel Fellow, Advanced Packaging Technology and Manufacturing) | **Source**: Semiconductor Engineering (Intel Foundry Sponsor Blog)

**Citation**: "The Heterogeneous Integration of Electronic Components," *Nature Reviews Electrical Engineering* 3, 254–263, March 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s44287-026-00270-1

---

## Summary

A global research team led by Intel published a perspective in *Nature Reviews Electrical Engineering* (March 2026) arguing that heterogeneous integration (HI) is now **essential to sustaining progress in AI, smartphones, and next-generation wireless communications**, but that existing industry roadmaps have not evolved at the same pace — creating coordination gaps that could hinder the future electronics ecosystem.

## Key Co-Authors / Institutions

- **Industry**: Intel, ASE (US) Inc., Texas Instruments, Third Millennium Test Solutions (3MTS), Boeing
- **Academia**: University of Toronto, Binghamton University, Penn State University, Arizona State University, UIUC, Purdue University, UCLA
- **Research Labs**: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, National Laboratory of the Rockies

## Core Argument

AI computing demands are doubling every three months, driven by constant data shuttling between processors and memory. Until this memory-bandwidth bottleneck is addressed, faster AI hardware will continue to come at steep environmental and economic cost. HI addresses this via vertical integration — stacking chips with dense, fine-pitch interconnects that dramatically shorten data-movement distances, reducing both latency and energy consumption.

## Technology Building Blocks Requiring Roadmap Attention

### 1. Hybrid Bonding
- Already enabling major gains in chip-to-chip interconnect density, bandwidth, and energy efficiency
- But introduces **new reliability challenges at bonding interfaces** — pointing to need for further research and refined design methodologies

### 2. Power Delivery
- Has become a **limiting factor** as AI workloads scale
- Motivates integration of efficient voltage regulation within the package using technologies such as **gallium nitride (GaN)**

### 3. Thermal Management
- Vertical integration intensifies thermal constraints as transistor counts approach the trillion-device range
- Drives interest in **liquid cooling embedded within chip stacks**
- Requires **co-design of power, performance, and thermal management as a unified system**

### 4. Thermo-Mechanical Reliability
- Increased integration and reduced feature sizes require more focus on ensuring reliability of complex heterogeneous structures
- Need for **modeling thermo-mechanical states of packages**, anticipating failure modes, and developing manufacturable solutions

## Critique of Current Roadmaps

The **IEEE Heterogeneous Integration Roadmap 2024** is acknowledged as valuable but has limitations:
1. Lacks **quantified, time-bound projections** for critical technology attributes
2. Does not consistently articulate **strategic research challenges** required to achieve long-term system goals

## Proposed Solution

The authors call for:
- **Rolling 20-year roadmaps** with device- and application-level projections
- Incorporation of **digital twins** to model performance and reliability prior to fabrication
- Explicit links between system needs and improvements in materials, processes, and design tools

## Application Areas

1. **AI accelerators**: Memory bandwidth bottleneck demands HI
2. **5G/6G infrastructure**: Millimeter-wave radios drive extreme integration density
3. **Aerospace and harsh environments**: Commercial packaging approaches must be qualified for long operational lifetimes
4. **Photonics (future)**: Integration of photonics promises to lift longstanding bandwidth and energy constraints — potentially redefining data centers
