Just days after unveiling the first High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) standard specifications with SK hynix in early August, Sandisk offered further details at its August 13 Investor Day. According to notes from Mizuho Securities and Goldman Sachs, the company has already taped out its first HBF product, with initial samples slated for 2027 and mass production expected to begin in 2028.

A Goldman Sachs note highlights HBF's potential to tackle the AI "memory wall" by combining HBM-class read bandwidth with 8–16× greater capacity. Sandisk's simulations suggest that an HBF-only architecture could deliver the same token output with half the GPU capacity required by an HBM-only setup.

Sandisk's analysis suggests four GPUs equipped with HBF delivered the same token output as eight HBM-based GPUs when running large AI models. The result suggests HBF could ease memory-capacity constraints, reduce the need for additional GPUs, and ultimately lower AI inference infrastructure costs.

HBF is a memory technology that stacks NAND flash instead of DRAM and places it close to computing devices such as GPUs. While targeting bandwidth close to HBM, HBF has higher latency as well as greater capacity at a lower cost.

Mizuho Securities believes HBF is unlikely to replace HBM outright, but could enable disaggregated architectures for AI inference, while the HBF consortium — which includes Google, Meta, SK hynix, and Tenstorrent — is working to advance this approach.

TrendForce notes that although NVIDIA has yet to announce any plans to adopt the technology, industry sentiment remains broadly positive. TrendForce believes the long-term architecture will likely combine HBM for ultra-high-speed computation with HBF for high-density data storage, creating a hybrid memory hierarchy that could become a key enabler of large-scale AI commercialization.

## HBF Standard Specifications (from FMS 2026)

The inaugural HBF specification (unveiled by SK hynix at FMS 2026) supports:
- Capacities: up to 512GB using 8-high and 16-high NAND stack configurations
- Three bandwidth tiers (Grade 1–3): roughly 0.4 TB/s to 3.0 TB/s
- Interface: OCP/UCIe compatible

## Sandisk Financial Outlook

Reuters, citing Sandisk's Investor Day materials, reports that the company expects:
- Revenue growth: mid-to-high-teens rate from FY28 through FY30
- Adjusted gross margins: ~80% sustained
- New Business Model (NBM) agreements with 8 customers, including 3 US hyperscalers
- NBM covers ~50% of storage output in FY2027, ~two-thirds in FY2028

## HBF Ecosystem Status

- Consortium members: Google, Meta, SK hynix, Tenstorrent (confirmed support)
- NVIDIA: not yet committed to HBF adoption
- Sandisk tape-out: completed (first product, details not disclosed)
- SK hynix: V10 NAND (375 layers) planned for 2027 for HBF stacking
