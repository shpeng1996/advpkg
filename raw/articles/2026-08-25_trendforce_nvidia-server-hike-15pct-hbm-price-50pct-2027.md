NVIDIA is reportedly planning to raise prices for Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell servers by more than 15% in early 2027, according to Bloomberg. The move could give major memory suppliers additional leverage in HBM pricing negotiations, with analysts expecting HBM contract prices to rise by more than 50% next year from 2026 levels, potentially benefiting Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

The pricing momentum is expected to span the full HBM lineup, from HBM3e to HBM4 and HBM4e. UBS projects 2027 HBM ASPs to rise by about 79% from 2026 levels, citing HBM4 and HBM4e pricing proving much stronger than initially expected.

## NVIDIA's Price Hikes Open More Room for HBM Pricing

NVIDIA's price hike plans suggest the tech giant has opted to pass the mounting burden of higher memory prices on to major customers such as Microsoft, Google and Oracle.

Fubon Research estimates that even if the cost of HBM4 used in Rubin systems nearly doubles from HBM3e, NVIDIA could still maintain a gross margin of around 75%–80% by raising system prices accordingly. That leaves NVIDIA with considerable room to absorb further HBM cost increases.

## DRAM Profitability Flip Sets the Stage for HBM Price Surge

The sharp rise in commodity DRAM prices is another key catalyst. Some DDR5 prices have nearly quadrupled over the past year, while HBM profitability is estimated to trail DDR5 by roughly 40 percentage points.

HBM prices are typically locked in through annual contracts, leaving them slower to reflect market swings. After DDR5 prices surged in H2 2025, wafer-level revenue and profitability for 64GB RDIMMs surpassed HBM in Q1 2026.

HBM's lower production efficiency further strengthens the case for higher pricing. Allocating more capacity to HBM generates smaller output increases, meaning makers give up more profitable commodity DRAM capacity.

## Memory Supply Shapes NVIDIA's HBM Choices

NVIDIA is considering multiple HBM configurations for its Rubin Ultra platform:
- Originally planned: 12-Hi HBM4e
- Under evaluation: 8-Hi HBM4e, 12-Hi HBM4, and 8-Hi HBM4

The shift reflects concerns over tight DRAM supply through 2027 and uncertainty surrounding 12-Hi HBM4e yield ramp.

The supply squeeze extends beyond HBM into server DRAM, adding cost pressure across the AI infrastructure stack. AI server makers must secure GPUs, HBM, and increasing volumes of server DRAM simultaneously.
