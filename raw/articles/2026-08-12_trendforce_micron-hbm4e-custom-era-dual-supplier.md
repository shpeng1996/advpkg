Memory giants are increasingly turning to custom HBM as the next growth engine. At the KeyBanc Technology Leadership Forum 2026, Micron EVP Sumit Sadana said the shift toward custom HBM will begin with HBM4E, with the company planning to work with customers on custom SKUs.

More notably, Sadana pointed to a potential shake-up in the HBM supplier landscape. Given the time, R&D and cost involved in HBM design and qualification, he said customers are unlikely to work with all three HBM suppliers across every project—potentially pushing the market toward a two-supplier or even single-source model.

That would mark a significant shift from today's landscape, where SK hynix, Samsung and Micron have all begun supplying HBM4 to NVIDIA's Vera Rubin.

## HBM's Growing Capacity Squeeze

Micron EVP Sumit Sadana highlighted the power-efficiency edge of its HBM3E, which consumes 30% less power than the next-best offering while remaining in high-volume production. HBM's rapid ramp-up is also putting increasing pressure on conventional DRAM supply, further tightening an already constrained memory market.

Sadana reiterated the 3:1 HBM-to-DDR trade-off: producing 100 bits of HBM3E effectively requires sacrificing around 300 bits of DDR supply. The capacity squeeze is set to intensify with newer HBM generations — as Micron moves through HBM4 and into HBM4E, that ratio is expected to worsen to nearly 4:1.

## Custom HBM Gains Momentum Among Memory Leaders

At the J.P. Morgan 54th Annual Global Technology, Media and Communications Conference in May, Micron's Global Operations EVP Manish Bhatia flagged the company's custom HBM roadmap. The company's first HBM4E will be a JEDEC-standard product, with ramp-up scheduled for 2027.

While Micron is still using 1-beta DRAM for HBM4, Bhatia said the company is expected to transition to 1-gamma DRAM in the HBM4E era. He also confirmed that the logic dies for both standard and custom HBM4E are expected to be manufactured by TSMC.

## SK hynix "One Team" and Next Memory Strategy

SK hynix has launched a recruitment drive for its "Next Memory Strategy" division through its monthly "Highway" career hiring program. The team will focus on developing customer-tailored memory solutions and identifying new memory business opportunities.

That strategy is already taking shape through SK hynix's "One Team" model: unlike Samsung's integrated, in-house approach, SK hynix's approach brings together itself, NVIDIA, and TSMC, with NVIDIA defining the HBM requirements for its GPUs, SK hynix handling memory stacking, and TSMC manufacturing the logic die and handling packaging.
