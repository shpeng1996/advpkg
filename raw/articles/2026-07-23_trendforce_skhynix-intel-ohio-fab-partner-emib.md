# [News] SK hynix Reportedly Eyed as Potential Partner to Operate Intel's Ohio Fab After Denying Acquisition Plans

2026-07-23 | TrendForce (citing Semafor, Barron's, Economy Tribune, Green Economy News, Korea Herald Business)

While both Intel and SK hynix denied market chatters that the latter could work towards acquiring Team Blue's Ohio fab, the two parties could still be exploring collaboration opportunities. Semafor, citing sources familiar with the matter, reports that SK hynix is among the potential partners being considered to help operate the facility, rather than take full ownership. Discussions remain at an early stage.

## Intel-SK hynix Ties Could Extend Beyond Ohio Fab Talks

SK hynix and Intel could explore initiatives similar to SK hynix's HBM and advanced packaging partnership with TSMC. SK hynix is testing the integration of its HBM with Intel's EMIB-based 2.5D packaging technology, evaluating its potential to combine memory and system semiconductors.

As AI demand drives the need for advanced packaging capacity, Samsung and Micron are also assessing EMIB's compatibility and power efficiency, reflecting memory makers' broader push toward diversified packaging solutions amid the tight supply of TSMC's CoWoS.

## Intel Ohio Fab Strategic Background

Despite years of delays, Intel's Ohio project remains a key pillar of U.S. semiconductor manufacturing strategy. The company broke ground in 2022 with plans to invest US$28 billion, but after missing its original 2025 opening target, Intel pushed back the launch to 2030. The campus is designed to support Intel's 14A and future nodes, with room for up to eight fabs.

## Memory Makers' U.S. Manufacturing Push

- Samsung's Taylor plant (2nm) has secured orders from Tesla's AI5 and AI6 chips; expected HVM 2027
- SK hynix is investing nearly US$4 billion in West Lafayette, Indiana, for HBM advanced packaging; groundbreaking done; full operations scheduled H2 2028
- SK hynix HBM market share: 56.4% (IDC, Q1 2026)

## EMIB Multi-Customer Assessment (New Data Points)

This article is the first to confirm that Samsung and Micron—in addition to SK hynix—are actively evaluating Intel EMIB-based 2.5D packaging as an alternative to TSMC CoWoS. This tri-party interest in EMIB represents a significant broadening of Intel Foundry's packaging customer base.
