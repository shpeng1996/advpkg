SK hynix broke ground on its first advanced semiconductor packaging plant in the U.S. on Aug. 27. The company aims to complete the cleanroom by October 2028, with the facility slated to begin HBM4E mass production in the third quarter of 2029.

SK hynix's Indiana fab will serve as a back-end production hub for stacking and packaging HBM and testing its performance. Advanced wafers produced in Korea will be shipped to the site for advanced packaging and testing before the finished HBM products are supplied to U.S. customers. Annual production capacity: several hundred thousand wafers.

## Investment and Funding

- Total investment: more than $4 billion (~KRW 5.52 trillion)
- CHIPS Act federal funding: $458 million (finalized by U.S. Department of Commerce)
- Construction: currently in groundwork phase

## CEO Outlook: Tight Supply Through 2030

SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-Jung: tight memory supply to persist through end of 2030 despite aggressive capacity expansion. "There are currently no clear signs of a downturn."

SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won: customers are seeking 60%–100% more AI memory from SK hynix in 2027 than in 2026. "Demand is likely to continue outpacing supply through 2027."

## SK hynix–Kioxia Potential Cooperation

At the Aug. 27 groundbreaking, CEO Kwak Noh-Jung signaled potential deeper ties with Kioxia:
"We are carefully considering how we can work together to develop the NAND flash memory market for the benefit of our customers and suppliers."

Bain Capital's SPC has become Kioxia's largest shareholder (14.19%, 77.4M shares), ahead of Toshiba (14.06%).

Meanwhile, Kioxia and SanDisk plan to invest >$31 billion in Japan through 2032 for semiconductor capacity expansion (Kioxia: ¥1.8T / $11.3B Kitakami plant).

## Samsung Taylor Update

Samsung Taylor Plant 1 nearing completion; trial production potentially starting as early as September 2026. Plant 2 construction begins late 2026, mass production 2030.

## U.S. Tariff Pressure Context

Washington considering extending semiconductor tariffs to finished products (laptops, consumer electronics containing chips), adding urgency to U.S. manufacturing expansion by Korean chipmakers.
