Heat is emerging as one of the biggest bottlenecks for AI infrastructure. KAIST Professor Kim Joung-ho warned that growing thermal challenges could constrain AI expansion as both system semiconductors and memory increasingly adopt 3D stacking. AI-based design automation and digital twin technologies are highlighted as potential tools.

Memory stacking introduces particular thermal risk. Beyond HBM (stacked DRAM), Kim expects HBF (stacked NAND flash) and HBS (stacked SRAM) as next-generation AI memory technologies. He cited an "HBM Design AI Agent" in his laboratory as an example of AI-driven heat dissipation optimization.

Samsung's zHBM stacks memory vertically above AI accelerators, delivering over 10x the memory density of HBM5, 3x energy efficiency, and more than 50% lower thermal resistance.

TrendForce notes that TDP for individual AI chips from NVIDIA, AMD, and Google has surpassed 1 kW, while rack-scale systems now consume hundreds of kilowatts. Liquid cooling penetration among AI chips is projected to rise from ~33% (2025) to 53% (2026) and approach 60% (2027).

Seoul National University of Science and Technology Professor Kim Sung-dong identified CPO and STCO as key approaches to thermal management. CPO converts electrical signals into optical signals, improving performance and power efficiency while reducing heat. STCO optimizes overall system performance including thermal management.

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and deep reinforcement learning are emerging as tools to autonomously design and optimize substrate warpage and power delivery networks (PDNs).

Key data points:
- TDP for individual AI chips (NVIDIA, AMD, Google): surpassed 1 kW
- Rack-scale AI system power: hundreds of kilowatts
- Liquid cooling penetration: ~33% (2025) -> 53% (2026) -> ~60% (2027)
- Samsung zHBM: >10x HBM5 memory density, 3x energy efficiency, >50% lower thermal resistance
- Next-gen AI memory hierarchy: HBM (DRAM) -> HBF (NAND) -> HBS (SRAM) expected
