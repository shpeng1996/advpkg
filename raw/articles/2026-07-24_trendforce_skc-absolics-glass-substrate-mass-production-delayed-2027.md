# SKC Reportedly Pushes Glass Substrate Mass Production to 2027, Targets Final Validation by Year-end

**Source:** TrendForce (citing Business Post, News Tomato, TradingKey) | **Date:** 2026-07-24

While SKC and its U.S. subsidiary **Absolics** remain among the frontrunners in the glass substrate market, **Business Post** reports that delays in mass production, driven by changing customer requirements, have allowed competitors to close the gap.

## Key Update: Mass Production Delayed to 2027

According to the report, SKC is currently supplying **prototypes to multiple North American big tech companies** for performance validation, with plans to:
- Complete **final reliability testing** in 2026 (year-end)
- Move into **full-scale mass production in 2027**

The company had initially targeted the world's **first commercial glass substrate production by end-2026**, but the timeline has since been **pushed back to 2027**.

Supported by News Tomato: SKC began supplying **evaluation samples to global customers in April 2026** and plans to conduct full-scale reliability testing.

TradingKey notes: SKC has completed the **world's first dedicated glass substrate mass production facility** and is now supplying samples to major customers, including **AMD and AWS**.

## Customer Landscape

Global tech giants accelerating glass substrate adoption:
- **Apple, Amazon, AMD**: accelerating efforts to adopt glass substrates
- **Intel**: set a goal of transitioning **all semiconductor substrates to glass substrates by 2030**

## Competitors Closing the Gap

- **Samsung Electro-Mechanics**: ramping up efforts, mass production targets **after 2027**
- **LG Innotek**: mass production targets **after 2027**

## SKC Financial Status

- SKC's return to profitability hinges on successful glass substrate business ramp-up
- Business Post expects earnings to improve significantly once full-scale production begins in 2027
- SKC is estimated to have posted an **operating loss in the tens of billions of won in Q2 2026**
- **15 consecutive quarters of losses** since Q4 2023
