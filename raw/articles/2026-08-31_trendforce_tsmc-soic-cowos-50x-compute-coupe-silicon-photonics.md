TSMC is stepping up its technology roadmap to meet surging AI computing demand. According to TechNews, April Li, Global Head of AI and HPC Business Development at TSMC, said at SEMICON Taiwan 2026 that AI computing demand is growing fivefold annually, putting increasing pressure on compute capacity. In response, TSMC is integrating advanced process, 3DFabric, and silicon photonics into its HPC platform. Notably, the company expects SoIC and CoWoS integration to boost overall system computing performance to 50 times its 2024 level by 2029.

Li said heterogeneous integration is the only way to push beyond current computing limits. TSMC plans N2P-on-N3P 3D integration in 2026, followed by A14-on-A14 in 2029, with interconnect pitch shrinking to 4.5 microns.

## TSMC Advances COUPE and Silicon Photonics Design

TSMC is also continuing to advance its COUPE technology. Its 2–4-micron bonding pitch limits transmission loss at 112 Gbps to just 0.06 dB, compared with 1.38 dB for micro-bumps, helping reduce power consumption and latency to 10–20 nanoseconds.

TSMC's self-developed COUPE platform uses TSMC-SoIC bonding technology to integrate electronic integrated circuits (EICs) with photonic integrated circuits (PICs), while supporting both grating couplers (GCs) and edge couplers (ECs). To further scale bandwidth, TSMC is pursuing two development paths:
1. Increase per-channel speeds from 200 Gbps to more than 400 Gbps while expanding channel counts from 16 to over 128, lifting total bandwidth from 3.2 Tbps to more than 12.8 Tbps.
2. Expand wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) from a single wavelength to 4, 8, 16, and beyond.

TSMC has enabled wafer-level optical testing for edge-coupled devices, allowing defective dies to be screened out before costly CPO assembly. TSMC has introduced an optical PDK that enables electronic-photonic co-simulation, supporting Cadence and Synopsys tools.

## Silicon Photonics Gains Ground as CPO Nears Mass Production

Silicon photonics expected to account for more than 50% of the optical transceiver market by 2027. CPO represents the next step, with some suppliers expected to begin mass production in the second half of 2026.

TSMC Vice President of Advanced Packaging Technology and Service K.C. Hsu said optical transceiver sales rose 25% in 2025 and are expected to grow another 50% in 2026, while the high-end 100G-and-above market doubled in 2024 and expanded another 60% in 2025.

Taiwan's silicon foundry ecosystem is already capable of producing optical engines at scale. The bigger challenge to large-scale deployment now lies in lasers, optical fibers, fiber connectors, and product testing.
