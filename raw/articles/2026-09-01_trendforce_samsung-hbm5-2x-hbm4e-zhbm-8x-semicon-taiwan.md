Following Hot Chips 2026 in August, Samsung has revealed fresh details on its next-generation HBM and zHBM at SEMICON Taiwan 2026. On Sept. 1, Jangseok Choi, corporate vice president of Memory Product Planning at Samsung Electronics, said the company's HBM5 is targeting 2× the performance and 20% higher performance per watt versus HBM4E.

The performance gains will be paired with a 20% reduction in thermal resistance, helping HBM5 tackle the rising heat generated by increasingly powerful AI accelerators.

Samsung is taking HBM5 to a more advanced process, with the base die moving to its in-house 2nm node, from the 4nm process used for HBM4 and HBM4E. The memory giant is preparing 12-, 16- and 20-layer DRAM stacks for HBM5, with mass production expected around 2028, following HBM4E.

## zHBM Targets 8× HBM4E Performance

Samsung is developing zHBM, targeting 8× the performance and 3× the performance per watt of HBM4E. The company also aims to reduce thermal resistance by 75%–90%.

zHBM is a new architecture that differs from conventional HBM: it stacks memory directly on top of the processor instead of placing memory beside the AI accelerator (xPU). By shortening the distance data must travel, the architecture delivers higher bandwidth and improved power efficiency. zHBM is expected to be launched after 2029.

## zNAND-O Targets DRAM-Like Speed at NAND Scale

On the NAND front, Samsung is developing zNAND-O, targeted for sampling in 2028. It aims to deliver 10× the bit density of DRAM for large-scale data storage, while offering 7× the read bandwidth and power efficiency of NAND. The technology is designed to meet the growing AI demand for DRAM-like speed and NAND-like capacity, particularly from generative AI and LLMs.

## Samsung's CUBE Strategy

At the Memory Executive Summit ahead of SEMICON Taiwan 2026, Samsung outlined its "CUBE" framework for next-generation memory. The strategy centers on four priorities:
- **Capacity**: Expand memory vertically to increase capacity without enlarging the PCB footprint.
- **Utilization**: Optimize architecture and placement of logic and memory to reduce latency; 3D memory as more than just stacking layers.
- **Bandwidth**: Shorten die-to-die distances by replacing horizontal data paths with vertical high-speed channels.
- **Efficiency**: Minimize energy required to move each bit of data; maximize performance per watt.
