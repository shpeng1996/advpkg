SK hynix is reportedly exploring cooperation with Intel in advanced 2.5D packaging. Sources say SK hynix is conducting R&D on Intel's EMIB-based 2.5D packaging technology using its own HBM, while also reviewing related materials and component suppliers for future mass production. The company is testing the integration of HBM and logic semiconductors using EMIB-integrated substrates supplied by Intel.

TSMC's CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) technology has been facing severe supply constraints amid surging AI semiconductor demand, prompting major tech firms to view Intel's EMIB as a potential alternative.

From SK hynix's perspective, early R&D on Intel EMIB is strategically important: although the company does not directly mass produce 2.5D packaging, developing HBM with consideration for 2.5D packaging structures and characteristics can improve yields and stability. SK hynix is operating a small-scale domestic line dedicated to 2.5D packaging R&D.

## Intel Ramps EMIB Capacity as AI Chipmakers Explore Alternatives

Intel has begun placing full-scale equipment orders related to EMIB production capacity expansion, signaling secured major clients:
- **Google**: EMIB effectively confirmed for next-generation TPU v8e (targeted 2H 2027)
- **Meta**: Considering EMIB for MTIA AI training/inference accelerators
- **Marvell and MediaTek**: Reviewing potential EMIB adoption

## EMIB vs. CoWoS: Key Technical Tradeoffs

Per TrendForce analysis:

**EMIB Advantages:**
- Embeds small silicon bridges directly into substrate (no large, costly interposer)
- Simpler structure, improved manufacturing yields
- Reduced thermal-expansion mismatch (lower CTE mismatch → lower warpage)
- Cost ~50% of CoWoS

**EMIB Limitations:**
- Smaller bridge area and routing density constrain bandwidth
- Increased transmission distance → slightly higher latency vs. CoWoS
- More attractive to ASIC customers than GPU vendors (GPU requires extreme BW + ultra-low latency)

**Key Data Points:**
- SK Hynix: R&D on EMIB 2.5D packaging using own HBM (2026-05-11)
- Google: TPU v8e on EMIB (2H2027)
- Meta: Evaluating EMIB for MTIA
- Intel EMIB yield: ~90% (2026 milestone)
- EMIB cost vs. CoWoS: ~50%
