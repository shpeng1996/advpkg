Six months after SK hynix partnered with SanDisk to drive High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) standardization, the consortium has reached a key milestone by unveiling the first HBF standard specifications. The announcement came at the opening of Future of Memory and Storage (FMS) 2026, held at the Santa Clara Convention Center in California from Aug. 4–6.

## First HBF Standard Specifications (via Open Compute Project / OCP)

The inaugural specification supports:
- Capacities: up to 512GB using 8-high and 16-high NAND stack configurations
- Three bandwidth tiers (Grade 1–3): approximately 0.4 TB/s to 3.0 TB/s
- Interconnect: UCIe (industry-standard), enabling integration with GPUs and CPUs
- Defines: interface and electrical characteristics, HBF die-stacking reliability and packaging guidelines, software I/O requirements

## Ecosystem Support

SK hynix is rallying major backers: Google DeepMind and Tenstorrent confirmed as supporters. NVIDIA has not yet announced plans to adopt HBF.

## Technology Positioning (TrendForce analysis)

HBF is complementary to HBM, not competing:
- HBM: ultra-high-speed computation
- HBF: high-density data storage (new tier between HBM and SSDs)
- Commercialization demand expected to pick up around 2030
- Long-term architecture: HBM (hot) + HBF (warm) + SSD (cold) hybrid memory hierarchy

## SanDisk Roadmap

- HBF prototypes in 2H 2026 (Japan as production site candidate)
- Pilot line operation: year-end 2026
- Commercialization: 2027

## SK hynix V10 375-Layer NAND (FMS 2026 announcement)

- World's first 375-layer, 10th-generation (V10) 4D NAND
- Mass production: early 2027
- Performance: 2.5x improvement in performance per watt
- Completes SK hynix full-stack AI memory portfolio: HBM (DRAM) + HBF (intermediate) + eSSD (storage)
