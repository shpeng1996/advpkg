## Summary of Key Items in SemiEngineering Chip Industry Week in Review #151 (2026-08-15)

### Advanced Packaging Equipment

**Applied Materials packaging revenue >70% growth in 2026:**
Applied Materials expects revenue from its advanced-packaging equipment business to grow more than 70% in FY2026, driven by investment in HBM and 3D chiplet stacking. This was announced alongside Q3 FY2026 earnings (published 2026-08-14). This upgrade from the prior >50% estimate signals faster-than-expected adoption of packaging-specific equipment across the AI supply chain.

**Lam Research $3B R&D expansion:**
Lam Research will spend $3 billion over 5 years to expand its R&D lab network, increasing experiment capacity by more than 50%. Lam Research and NY Creates are partnering to train approximately 3,500 university students in semiconductor process integration over the next five years, deploying Lam's SEMulator3D virtual fabrication software.

### Industry Funding and Expansion

**South Korea $3.5B semiconductor fund:**
The South Korean government is creating a ~$3.5B semiconductor fund targeting promising materials, components, equipment, and fabless companies, alongside $3.5B in trade financing for export-oriented suppliers.

**ASE SPIL Douliu plant (confirmation):**
ASE's subsidiary SPIL broke ground on a nearly US$3.1B (~TWD 100B) advanced packaging and test plant in Douliu, Taiwan, expected to add CoWoS capacity for AI chips with first-phase production targeted for 2028. (Previously collected in raw/articles/2026-08-12_trendforce_ase-spil-douliu-twd100b-cowos-2028.md)

**Discovered Materials:**
Discovered Materials launched with $9M to build AI agents that discover new materials for semiconductor chips.

### Research Advances

**KAIST programmable dynamic memtransistor:**
KAIST developed a "programmable dynamic memtransistor," with researchers claiming reduced prediction errors by up to 40-fold versus conventional devices.

### Geopolitics

**China legacy chip capacity approaching 50% of global output by 2030:**
China is rapidly gaining ground across the semiconductor supply chain, with legacy-chip capacity projected to approach half of global output by 2030. This reinforces the geopolitical pressure on advanced packaging capacity outside China.
